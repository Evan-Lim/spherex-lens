          SPHERExLens · Documentation

          Pipeline Overview & Reference

          SPHERExLens compares pictures of the same patch of sky taken months apart and points at whatever changed: a star that moved, a star that got brighter or fainter, or an asteroid passing through.

          Instrument

          NASA's SPHEREx mission surveys the whole sky again and again in 102 infrared colours (0.75–5.0 µm).

          Pipeline Steps

          1. Ingest: Fetch SPHEREx Level-2 cutouts from NASA/IPAC IRSA and the star catalogues for the field (Gaia DR3, AllWISE, VSX).
          2. Co-add: Throw away bad pixels and stack the exposures of each visit into one sharper picture.
          3. Subtract: Subtract one visit from the next (ZOGY). What is left is what changed.
          4. Triage: For every change above 5σ, ask the catalogues “do you know this star?”, check whether it moves the way Gaia says it should, look at its NEOWISE history, and give it a verdict.
          5. Asteroid check: Ask NASA JPL (SB Identification) and the Canadian SSOIS service which known asteroids were at that spot at that time. A match gives the verdict Known asteroid; a moving-object signal with no match becomes Possible new asteroid. If the services cannot be reached the card says asteroid check unavailable — treat such a detection with caution.
          6. Scores: A machine-learning model gives each detection an ML score (how likely it is real). It only ranks detections so the best are looked at first — it never decides. The evidence score adds up every check (ML, motion, asteroid check, NEOSSat) into one number from 0 to 100.
          7. Validate: Plant fake sources in real images and count how many come back (injection-recovery); run the same pipeline on sky with nothing in it to count false alarms. The results are on the Accuracy page.

          Map Legend

          • KNOWN Matched to a catalogued star, variable, or mover.
          • CANDIDATE No catalog match within radius.
          • KNOWN ASTEROID A catalogued asteroid was at this spot at this time (diamond marker).
          • POSSIBLE NEW ASTEROID Behaves like an asteroid but no known one is there (diamond marker).
          • GOT FAINTER A source that dimmed between visits (square with a minus sign).
          • TARGET Primary field target position.
          1. Select Field: Pick a registered target from the toolbar dropdown or enter custom RA / Dec in degrees and click Load field from IRSA.
          2. Coverage chips: The row under the four numbers says which archives cover this field — for example SPHEREx ✓ 3 epochs · NEOWISE ✓ 23 visits · NEOSSat ✗. A ✗ means that archive has nothing here, so its check could not help.
          3. Inspect Sky Viewer: Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, click a marker to fill the Triage Verdict panel. Click the same marker again (or press Vet in the Detections table) to open its vetting card.
          4. Vetting card: Before / difference pictures cut out around the detection, the asteroid check, NEOSSat, the ML and evidence scores with their reasons, and buttons to open the spot in WiseView, the IRSA Finder Chart, SIMBAD and Gaia at MAST.
          5. Vote: Under Is this real? choose Real, Bogus or Not sure. Help classify in the toolbar lists the detections the computer is least sure about. Votes need the live backend; on the pre-rendered site the buttons say so.
          6. Public / Expert: Public shows plain-language verdicts. Expert adds the ML and evidence columns, pipeline and model versions, and downloads (CSV, VOTable, per-field JSON, Simple Cone Search). Your choice is remembered on this device.
          7. Accuracy: The Accuracy button shows how often the pipeline is right, measured on planted sources and empty sky.
          8. Switch Layers: Toggle between Co-add, Difference D, and Significance S, or switch to All-sky view.
          9. Replay Snapshots: In the Sky Viewer toolbar, choose a previous published version from Snapshot, or press Play to step oldest to latest. Select a candidate or known object first to follow its dipole/monopole with the purple trail.
          10. Utility Windows: Open History, Statistics, or Recovery from the toolbar as draggable floating panels.
          11. Keyboard: Tab moves through every control; Esc closes the open window; on the page +/- zoom, 0 fits, g grid, d detections.
          Field
          9′×9′ cutout region (3″/px).
          Epoch
          Grouped SPHEREx visits at a distinct observation date.
          σ (sigma)
          Significance in background noise units (σ1).
          KNOWN
          Detection matched to Gaia DR3, AllWISE, or VSX.
          CANDIDATE
          Unmatched detection >5σ.
          Known asteroid
          JPL (and usually SSOIS) says a catalogued asteroid was right there. Expected, not a discovery.
          Possible new asteroid
          Seen in one visit only, moving-object-like, and no known asteroid there. Needs follow-up.
          Got fainter
          A monopole with a negative sign: the source was brighter in the first visit than in the second.
          ML score
          Machine-learning estimate (0–100%) that a detection is real. It ranks detections; it does not decide.
          Evidence score
          All checks combined into one 0–100 number, with each part shown.
          NEOSSat
          Canada's small space telescope; if it took pictures of the spot, we say whether the source shows up there too.
          Coverage chip
          One archive and whether it has data for this field (✓ yes, ✗ no).
          Vote
          Your Real / Bogus / Not sure answer. Votes on the uncertain detections help train the next model.
          Precision / recall
          Of what we flag, how much is real / of what is real, how much we flag.
          Recovery grid
          Synthetic injection completeness across SNR and μ.
          RA / Dec
          ICRS right ascension and declination in decimal degrees.
