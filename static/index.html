            SPHERExLens · Documentation

            Pipeline Overview & Reference

            SPHERExLens compares pictures of the same patch of sky taken months apart and points at whatever changed: a star that moved, a star that got brighter or fainter, or an asteroid passing through.

            Instrument

            NASA's SPHEREx mission surveys the whole sky again and again in 102 infrared colours (0.75–5.0 µm).

            Pipeline Steps

            1. Ingest: Fetch SPHEREx Level-2 cutouts from NASA/IPAC IRSA and the star catalogues for the field (Gaia DR3, AllWISE, VSX).
            2. Co-add: Throw away bad pixels and stack the exposures of each visit into one sharper picture.
            3. Subtract: Subtract one visit from the next (ZOGY). What is left is what changed.
            4. Triage: For every change above the detection threshold, ask the catalogues “do you know this star?”, check whether it moves the way Gaia says it should, look at its NEOWISE history, and give it a verdict.
            5. Asteroid check: Ask NASA JPL (SB Identification) and the Canadian SSOIS service which known asteroids were at that spot at that time. A match gives the verdict Known asteroid; a moving-object signal with no match becomes Possible new asteroid.
            6. Scores: A machine-learning model gives each detection an ML score (how likely it is real) to rank the best candidates first. The evidence score combines every check (ML, motion, asteroid check, NEOSSat) into a 0–100 score.
            7. Validate: Plant fake sources in real images to measure completeness (injection-recovery) and test empty sky to count false alarms.

            Map Legend

            • KNOWN Matched to a catalogued star, variable, or mover.
            • CANDIDATE No catalog match within radius.
            • KNOWN ASTEROID A catalogued asteroid was at this spot at this time (diamond marker).
            • POSSIBLE NEW ASTEROID Behaves like an asteroid but no known one is there (diamond marker).
            • GOT FAINTER A source that dimmed between visits (square with a minus sign).
            • TARGET Primary field target position.
            1. Select Field: Pick a registered target from the top bar or enter custom RA / Dec in degrees and click Fetch IRSA. Press ⌘K / Ctrl+K to jump anywhere instantly.
            2. Inspect Sky Viewer: Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, click a marker to inspect it, click again to open its Vetting Card. Use Blink, Wipe, Z-Scale, and Ruler to inspect dipoles and intensity cuts pixel-by-pixel.
            3. Filter & Curate: Use the Detections filter chips, SNR/ML sliders, and sortable headers to narrow candidates. Star any interesting object to pin it to your Watchlist with analyst notes and CSV/JSON export.
            4. Vote: In the Vetting Card, compare the cutouts against the visual reference stamps and vote Real, Bogus, or Not sure (or press 1/2/3).

            Field
            9′×9′ cutout region (3″/px).
            Epoch
            Grouped SPHEREx visits at a distinct observation date.
            σ (sigma)
            Significance in background noise units (σ1).
            ZOGY
            Zackay, Ofek & Gal-Yam optimal image subtraction that accounts for PSF differences between epochs.
            Dipole
            A side-by-side dark and bright spot in a difference image — signature of a moving star or slight alignment shift.
            KNOWN
            Detection matched to Gaia DR3, AllWISE, or VSX.
            CANDIDATE
            Unmatched detection above the detection threshold.
            Known asteroid
            JPL (and usually SSOIS) confirms a catalogued asteroid at that spot and time.
            Possible new asteroid
            Seen in one visit only, moving-object-like, and no known asteroid there. Needs follow-up.
            ML score
            Machine-learning estimate (0–100%) that a detection is real. It ranks detections; it does not decide.
            Evidence score
            All checks combined into one 0–100 number, with each part shown.
            NEOSSat
            Canada's small space telescope; checked for follow-up frames of candidates.
            RA / Dec
            ICRS right ascension and declination in decimal degrees.
